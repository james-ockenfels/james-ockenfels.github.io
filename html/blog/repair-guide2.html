Writing a Second Repair Guide for the New Chromebook Fleet

With the school district transitioning to brand new hardware models from a different manufacturer, I realized future interns would need a completely fresh set of instructions.

Next school year, after I have graduated, our district will be rolling out brand new student Chromebooks. These devices are built by Acer instead of HP, meaning next year's cohort will be facing completely unfamiliar hardware without any experienced senior interns around to guide them through repairs.

This year, every single intern is a senior. There are seven of us, and none of us will be here next year. Whatever we know about troubleshooting and working on student devices walks out the door when we graduate unless we proactively document it. Because of this structural shift, I sat down to write an entirely new guide.

Why It Was Needed

I have already written one comprehensive repair manual for the devices we have been servicing for years, the HP 14 G7 models. However, these new incoming devices are engineered and designed entirely differently by Acer, making my previous documentation obsolete for the new fleet.

In addition, fresh hardware models always present a bit of a mystery because we do not yet know their common failure points. While the older HP models frequently suffered from recurring camera module failures, we do not know if these Acer models will share those exact same vulnerabilities, nor do we know the most optimal assembly shortcuts yet. Although this new initial guide cannot predict every specific future hardware failure, it establishes a clear structural layout so that new procedures and diagnostic notes can be added seamlessly by the next team.

What's In It

The manual outlines 10 foundational hardware issues alongside step-by-step repair breakdowns. It covers bottom cover removal, battery replacement, keyboard replacement, touchpad replacement, screen replacement, display cable swapping, camera replacement, top shell replacement, and factory powerwashing procedures. Each independent section includes prominent warning blocks right at the top so technicians spot critical mechanical steps before starting, rather than halfway through when components are already at risk.

The layout also features a quick reference index table at the front that maps physical symptoms directly to the corresponding repair section, parts layouts gathered from official documentation, a core tools inventory list, escalation workflow guidelines, and general hardware tips. My goal was to create a reference document that someone with zero prior repair experience could pick up on day one and immediately use to resolve problems.

Who Is Going to Be Using It

While it was primarily designed to guide next year's incoming student interns, it will also serve as a reference for other district IT staff members who have equally little hands-on experience dealing with this specific Acer hardware lifecycle.

What Writing It Taught Me

Drafting detailed technical documentation forces you to build an absolute understanding of your own workflows. There were several procedures I assumed I knew perfectly, but slowing down to write them out forced me to evaluate the exact, logical order of operations. It also made me recognize just how much ambient context I had absorbed during my internship without consciously realizing it.

Tricks like using a magnetic bit to simulate a laptop lid closing during open-bench testing, or verifying system functionality completely before screwing the outer casing back together, are easy to take for granted once they become second nature. Effective technical documentation must be written directly for the perspective of someone who does not know what you know. This is a difficult writing balance to strike, which is why the version 2.0 label exists on the cover page. I heavily revised the material after collecting peer feedback to resolve anything that lacked absolute clarity on the first pass.

Why I'm Proud of It

Of everything I have accomplished throughout this IT internship, these documentation initiatives are what I am most proud of. Performing day-to-day hardware repairs is incredibly valuable, but engineering resources that outlast your personal tenure to make life easier for the next group feels like a genuine professional contribution. This guide exists simply because I identified an operational gap and took the initiative to fix it.
